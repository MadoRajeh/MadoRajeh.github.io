PayList

A product built to sell, not a client project. Small businesses in the Philippines collect a lot of money on repeat (rent, tuition, subscriptions, instalments), and chase most of it by hand over Messenger. PayList does the chasing.

The problem

Chasing payments is a job nobody schedules

The owner knows who owes what. What they don't have is a system that remembers to ask, asks the same way every time, and gives the customer a straight answer to "okay, how do I actually pay you?". So it gets done late, unevenly, or not at all, and the awkwardness of asking gets in the way of the asking.

The engineering

The decisions that actually mattered

It is deliberately not a payment processor

Money moves straight from the customer to the business. PayList holds no funds and touches no card details. That decision removes an entire category of licensing, compliance and liability, and it is why a one-person business can actually use it.

Sending twice is worse than sending late

Every reminder writes to a send log before it goes out, keyed on the invoice and the reminder stage. If the scheduler runs twice, or a deploy restarts it mid-run, the second attempt finds the log entry and does nothing. Nagging a customer twice for the same peso costs more trust than being an hour late.

Every tenant is walled off in the database itself

Separation is enforced by row-level security in Postgres, not by remembering to add a WHERE clause in application code. A query that forgets the tenant returns nothing rather than someone else's customer list.

The customer never makes an account

The pay page is a plain link with a signed token. No login, no app, no password reset for a person who just wants to see a QR code and send ₱2,400.

Free tiers all the way down

Every service in the stack has a real free tier, so the product can run at zero cost until it earns. A tool that costs money before it makes money never gets launched.

Where it landed

The outcome

Being straight about it: PayList records and chases. It does not process payments, reconcile bank feeds, or confirm that money arrived: the owner marks an invoice paid. That is a real limit, and it is the trade that keeps the product simple enough to ship and legal enough to sell without a payments licence.
