A twelve-month plan written as a pitch, where every quarter has one job, one measure, and one thing being stopped.

The problem

Most marketing plans are a list of everything

They add channels, tactics and ideas until the plan is a wish list nobody can execute or measure. Nothing is ever removed, so by the second quarter the team is doing all of it badly and none of it is attributable.

The engineering

The decisions that actually mattered

Sequence the quarters, don't parallelise them

Fix measurement, then cut what isn't working, then scale what is, then build the owned channel. Attempting all four at once produces four half-finished initiatives and no way of telling which one moved the number.

Nothing goes on without something coming off

Every quarter names a thing being stopped. A plan that only adds is a plan that will be quietly abandoned once the team runs out of hours, usually around month five.

Write the success measure before the tactics

Each quarter states what success looks like in one measurable line, agreed up front. Deciding after the fact whether a quarter went well is how every quarter goes well.

Show the measurement plan, not just the strategy

The plan ends with how it gets tracked — hourly dashboard, automatic weekly report, no manual exports anywhere in the chain. A strategy with no measurement attached is a document, not a plan.
