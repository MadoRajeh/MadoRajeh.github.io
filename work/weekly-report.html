The problem

Half a workday, every week, assembling numbers

Pull the figures, work the comparisons, spot what moved, write it up, format it, send it. Necessary work, done manually, in the same order, every single week, and always at the start of the week when the time is most expensive.

The engineering

The decisions that actually mattered

Report movement, not levels

A location sitting at target is not news. A location that moved eight percent in a week is. The report leads with change, because change is the only part anybody can act on.

The flags are rules, not opinions

What gets flagged is defined by thresholds set in advance: over target, under a conversion floor, worse for three weeks running. Nobody decides each Monday what counts as bad news, which is how bad news stops quietly not getting mentioned.

Always end with next week's actions

A report that stops at the numbers gets read and forgotten. Three specific actions at the bottom turns it into a thing that gets replied to.

Label which cost per lead you mean

Paid cost per lead and all-in cost per lead are different numbers and both are useful. Showing one and calling it "cost per lead" is how two people end up in a meeting quoting different figures from the same report.

The deliverable

Weekly performance report

One page, assembled every Monday at six: the week in four numbers, spend by channel, what got flagged, and what happens next.
